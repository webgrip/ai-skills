# Evidence: what the rules in this skill rest on

Strength labels: **Strong** (a specification, a peer-reviewed or controlled study, or the vendor's own documentation or source of its system) · **Consistent** (several independent sources agree) · **Contested** (sources point both ways) · **Anecdotal** (one case, one estate, a design default with no published calibration). Sources were read in October 2026; prices, versions and product behaviour move, so re-check before quoting a number.

## FinOps method

- **Framework structure.** Phases Inform, Optimize, Operate; domains and capabilities as listed in [framework.md](framework.md#phases-and-readers) ([FinOps Framework](https://www.finops.org/framework/), Strong). The framework carries no version number; the page's latest update was dated March 2026.
- **Reporting maturity.** Crawl reports actual spend and trend only, Walk adds projected versus actual, Run surfaces variance when a threshold is breached; one success measure is tagging compliance above 90 % ([Reporting & Analytics](https://www.finops.org/framework/capabilities/reporting-analytics/), Strong). → every dashboard here carries a projection; the linter's `missing-forecast` rule.
- **Maturity targets.** Allocation: at least 70 % of cost to a known owner at Crawl, 85 % at Walk, above 90 % at Run. Forecast accuracy variance: below 20 %, below 10 %, below 5 % ([Maturity model](https://www.finops.org/framework/maturity-model/), Strong). Budget variance: 20 %, 15 %, 12 % ([Budgeting](https://www.finops.org/framework/capabilities/budgeting/), Strong). These are two different measures; secondary sources mix them up.
- **Showback and chargeback.** Showback is always required; chargeback posts cost into budgets and P&L ([Invoicing & Chargeback](https://www.finops.org/framework/capabilities/invoicing-chargeback/), Strong).
- **Unit economics** examples (per transaction, per customer, per GB stored, per token) and the Walk step of fully loaded cost ([Unit Economics](https://www.finops.org/framework/capabilities/unit-economics/), Strong).
- **Allocation hygiene** KPIs: share of cost that cannot be allocated, share with allocation metadata; at Run, ownership metadata is a precondition for provisioning ([Allocation](https://www.finops.org/framework/capabilities/allocation/), Strong).
- **FOCUS.** v1.4 is the current release; providers list their export version on the FOCUS site (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud at 1.2). BilledCost is the invoiced cost and is 0 for usage fully covered by a commitment; EffectiveCost is the amortized cost and is 0 on the covering purchase row; summed over related rows they are equal ([FOCUS v1.4 BilledCost](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/columns/cost-and-usage/billed-cost/), [EffectiveCost](https://focus.finops.org/docs/specification/v1-4/columns/cost-and-usage/effective-cost/), [providers](https://focus.finops.org/), Strong). The v1.4 ratification date was seen only in search snippets (unverified). The ServiceName, ResourceId, Tags, quantity and BillingCurrency definitions were not opened; the skill uses them by name only.
- **Amortization.** Without it, reserved-instance fees show as a spike on the day charged; AWS's worked example spreads $30 upfront plus $2.48 per month to $0.165 per day ([AWS Cost Explorer](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cost-management/latest/userguide/ce-advanced.html), Strong).

## Forecasts and anomalies

- **Forecasting maturity.** Walk is rolling and trend-based, Run combines rolling, trend-based and driver-based and tracks against amortized cost; KPIs are forecast accuracy and drift ([Forecasting](https://www.finops.org/framework/capabilities/forecasting/), Strong).
- **Linear extrapolation overshoots capped billing.** Shown on one inventory: € 39,31 linear against € 32,96 actual rule on the 25th ([case](case-unfold-hetzner.md), Anecdotal as one estate; the mechanism follows from the billing rule and is reproduced by the example files).
- **The daily band** (± 1.96 × sd × √remaining days) assumes independent, roughly normal daily costs. Cost series are autocorrelated and skewed, so the band is an indication, not a calibrated interval (Anecdotal, design default).
- **Anomaly management** measures time to detect, time to notify and how long anomalies stay unresolved ([Anomaly Management](https://www.finops.org/framework/capabilities/anomaly-management/), Strong).
- **Vendor detectors** all model an expected baseline: AWS (ML with seasonality, about three runs a day, 10 days of history for a new service), Azure (daily forecast from the last 60 days, flag outside a confidence interval), Google (deviation percentage plus absolute cost impact) ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/cost-management/latest/userguide/manage-ad.html), [Azure](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/understand/analyze-unexpected-charges), [Google](https://docs.cloud.google.com/billing/docs/how-to/manage-anomalies), Strong as each vendor's documentation of its own system). → relative and absolute thresholds together.
- **Modified z-score.** M = 0.6745 (x − median) / MAD, flag |M| > 3.5 (Iglewicz and Hoaglin 1993); the plain z-score is misleading on small samples because its maximum is (n−1)/√n ([NIST e-Handbook 1.3.5.17](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda35h.htm), Strong). The 3.5 threshold is a recommendation for roughly normal data, not calibrated for cost series (Anecdotal for this use).

## Billing sources

- **Hetzner Cloud**: hourly billing rounded up to the started hour and capped at the monthly price; servers billed while off; only outgoing traffic billed in 100 MB blocks; snapshots per GB-month; backups 20 % of the server price; primary IPv6 free, IPv4 billed while it exists; invoices up to 28 days after month end ([Billing FAQ](https://docs.hetzner.com/cloud/billing/faq/), [Billing system](https://docs.hetzner.com/general/billing-and-account-management/billing-at-hetzner/billing-system-hetzner/), [IPv4 pricing](https://docs.hetzner.com/general/others/ipv4-pricing/), Strong). No cost or invoice endpoint; `GET /pricing` returns net and gross prices ([API spec](https://docs.hetzner.cloud/cloud.spec.json), Strong). Price adjustment of 15 June 2026 ([notice](https://docs.hetzner.com/general/infrastructure-and-availability/price-adjustment/), Strong). Per-GB prices for volumes and snapshots and the traffic overage price were not found on pages that rendered (unverified): read them from the API.
- **Hetzner dedicated**: monthly plus setup fee (Strong, price notice); Server Auction without setup fee ([FAQ](https://docs.hetzner.com/robot/general/server-auction-faqs), Strong). Hourly billing of dedicated servers: Contested between Hetzner pages.
- **OpenCost**: allocation fields and idle row ([API](https://opencost.io/docs/integrations/api), Strong); metrics list ([metrics](https://opencost.io/docs/integrations/metrics), Strong); `EMIT_KSM_V1_METRICS` defaults to on in the source (Strong, source at main) and was observed producing duplicate `kube_*` series under `job="opencost"` in one cluster (Anecdotal, live query). Kubecost idle = cluster cost − allocated CPU, RAM and GPU cost, allocation = max(request, usage) ([IBM Kubecost docs](https://www.ibm.com/docs/en/SSW0JQG_2.x/using-kubecost/navigating-the-kubecost-ui/cost-allocation/efficiency-idle.html), Strong).
- **LiteLLM**: spend log columns, daily tables and budget fields (Strong, source at main); Prometheus metric names ([docs](https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/proxy/prometheus), Strong). Whether `/metrics` needs an Enterprise licence differs between releases (Contested over time): check the deployed tag.
- **VAT**: reverse charge for cross-border B2B services (Directive 2006/112/EC art. 44 and 196, Strong); Dutch rates 21 %, 9 %, 0 % ([business.gov.nl](https://business.gov.nl/finance-and-taxes/vat/vat-rates-and-exemptions), Strong); consumer place of supply for electronic services (Consistent, article not opened).
- **Electricity**: CBS average variable supply tariff and the Rijksoverheid energy-tax figure (11,1 ct per kWh incl. VAT) give a marginal price near € 0,26 per kWh in 2026 ([CBS](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/maatwerk/2026/42/verdeling-energietarieven-2026), [Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/energie-thuis/vraag-en-antwoord/opbouw-energierekening), Strong for the components; the sum is computed). The common rule of thumb "1 W ≈ € 3 per year" implies about € 0,34 per kWh: inside the contract range, above the average. → price as a parameter.

## Grafana and query engines

- **Currency units**: `currencyEUR` abbreviates and always uses a `.` decimal; `currency:financial:€` gives full digits through `Intl.NumberFormat` in the browser's locale ([standard options](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/panels-visualizations/configure-standard-options/), [source](https://github.com/grafana/grafana/blob/main/packages/grafana-data/src/valueFormats/categories.ts), Strong). No organisation-wide number locale ([issue 4252](https://github.com/grafana/grafana/issues/4252), open). The nl-NL rendering of `currency:financial:€` is inferred from the code, not observed (unverified).
- **Automatic decimals truncate**: the docs' example renders 100.456 as 100 (Strong). → `currency-decimals`.
- **Table footer**: 12.2 moved it to per-field `custom.footer.reducers`; the old `options.footer` is migrated in the browser on load ([what's new](https://grafana.com/whats-new/2025-09-03-improved-footer-for-table-visualization/), migration source, Strong). → `legacy-table-footer`.
- **Stat options** incl. `percentChangeColorMode: inverted` ([stat docs](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/panels-visualizations/visualizations/stat/), Strong).
- **Time**: "This month so far" is `now/M` to `now`; dashboard timezone takes `utc`, `browser` or an IANA name ([use dashboards](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/dashboards/use-dashboards/), Strong). `$__range` is supported for Prometheus and Loki (Strong).
- **Best practices**: tell a story, avoid sprawl, use descriptions, avoid unnecessary refresh ([Grafana best practices](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana/latest/dashboards/build-dashboards/best-practices/), Strong as vendor guidance).
- **PromQL** has no calendar month; `day_of_month()` and `days_in_month()` are UTC; MetricsQL adds `timezone_offset()` and its `increase` uses the sample before the window ([MetricsQL](https://docs.victoriametrics.com/victoriametrics/metricsql/), Prometheus function docs, Strong).
- **grafana-operator** CRD fields ([API reference](https://grafana.github.io/grafana-operator/docs/api/), Strong).

## Dashboard design

- **Dashboard definition and pitfalls**: single screen, at a glance; inadequate context (no comparison), excessive precision, indirect measures (show variance directly), inappropriate media (a pie you must read numbers from should be a table) ([Few, Common Pitfalls in Dashboard Design, 2006](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/Whitepapers/Common_Pitfalls.pdf), Strong as the primary text of expert guidance).
- **Excess precision vs the 2-decimal house rule**: Few prefers $3.8M to $3,848,305.93 in a headline (Contested against the owner's 2-decimal rule). → tables keep cents; headline rounding only with the exact figure beside it.
- **Bullet graph**: replaces gauges; featured measure, 1–2 comparative markers, 2–5 ranges in intensities of one hue so colour-blind readers can tell them apart; reversed order for "best when low" measures such as expenses ([Few, Bullet Graph Design Specification](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/misc/Bullet_Graph_Design_Spec.pdf), Strong as specification). Its claim to read faster than radial gauges is not tested in the spec (Consistent as expert opinion).
- **Graphical perception**: position on a common scale beats length, angle and area. Cleveland and McGill 1984 (JASA 79:531–554; ranking Consistent across secondary sources, paper not opened); the crowdsourced replication found position significantly better than length, area worse than angle, both worse than position, and square-treemap areas worst ([Heer and Bostock, CHI 2010](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jheer/files/2010-MTurk-CHI.pdf), Strong). → `pie-for-money`, `gauge-for-money`, sorted tables and bars.
- **Tables vs graphs**: cognitive fit, tables for symbolic look-up tasks and graphs for spatial tasks (Vessey 1991, Decision Sciences 22(2); Consistent, paper not opened). → stat and table for single values and comparisons, also the owner's stated preference.
- **Minimalism is contested**: Tufte's data-ink ratio (primary not opened); embellished charts were no worse for accuracy and better recalled ([Bateman et al., CHI 2010](https://vis.csail.mit.edu/classes/6.859/readings/pdfs/Bateman-UsefulJunk.pdf), Strong); unusual charts are more memorable ([Borkin et al. 2013](https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/20130101-what-makes-a-visualization-memorable), Strong). Memorability is not the goal of a monitoring screen (Contested for this use). → keep it plain, but do not cite "maximize data-ink" as law.
- **Variance notation**: IBCS uses red and green for variances only, solid actuals, outlined plans, hatched forecasts, vertical waterfalls for costs ([IBCS summary](https://www.ibcs.com/resource/top-and-bottom-5-of-international-business-communication-standards/), Consistent; the standard itself, ISO 24896, was not opened).
- **Colour vision deficiency**: about 1 in 12 men, red-green most common ([NEI](https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness), Strong); about 8 % of men and 0.4 % of women of European descent (Birch 2012, JOSA A 29(3); abstract only, Consistent). WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1 and 1.4.11 ([use of colour](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html), [non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html), Strong).

## The linter itself

- **Fixture pair and a passing case.** `test.sh` requires every rule to fire on `fixtures/careless` and none on `fixtures/careful`, runs unit tests for each exemption, and checks every rule is documented. Ten deliberate breakages each made it fail (a rule that never fires, a rule that over-fires, the monthly cap removed from the forecast, a rule undocumented, a crashing linter, CRD parsing disabled, and four exemptions removed), and the unmodified tree passed (Anecdotal, this repository).
- **False-positive pass on real dashboards.** 85 dashboards and 1,681 panels from one homelab Grafana. The first pass produced false positives from `topk` legends, token-price multipliers read as VAT, SQL that formats money as text, threshold lines, budget tiles and a tokenizer bug; each became an exemption with a unit test. The remaining findings were read and judged real ([case](case-unfold-hetzner.md#false-positive-pass), Anecdotal).
