# What keeps people watching a vertical Short: evidence

Scope: YouTube Shorts first, then TikTok, Reels and LinkedIn vertical video. The question is what stops a viewer swiping away and what keeps them watching.
Labels: **PEER** (peer-reviewed), **OFF** (official platform documentation or staff statement), **DATA** (large sample, correlational), **VENDOR** (vendor claim with no published method), **ANEC** (practitioner convention), **GAP** (repeated everywhere, not verifiable).
I opened every linked URL. Where I only saw an abstract or a secondary summary, the entry says so.
One caveat applies throughout: almost all peer-reviewed attention research is on **ads** (TV, pre-roll, TikTok ads), not on organic Shorts. Treat the transfer to organic Shorts as an assumption.

## 1. What YouTube actually measures (read this first)

- **OFF**: Since **2025-03-31**, a Shorts "view" is counted whenever a Short starts playing or replays, with no minimum watch time. The old metric survives as **"engaged views"** (YouTube Analytics, Advanced Mode), which YouTube describes as how many viewers choose to keep watching. YPP earnings use engaged views. Sources: [TeamYouTube post](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549/a-change-to-how-we-count-views-on-shorts?hl=en) (I could load only the title; details are from [TechCrunch 2025-03-26](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)).
- **OFF**: From **2026-08-24**, views count "the moment a video starts to play across all formats, including Shorts". Earnings stay on engaged views and engaged watch hours. ([YouTube Help 2991785](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785)) **Consequence:** the public view count no longer tells you anything about retention. Judge Shorts on engaged views, viewed-vs-swiped-away and average % viewed.
- **OFF (staff, 2023)**: Shorts product lead Todd Sherman said a view should "encode for your intent of watching that thing". Studio shows **viewed vs swiped away** per Short. He told creators to put storytelling ahead of any target length. Most Shorts traffic comes from the feed, so custom thumbnails matter little. ([SEJ summary, 2023-08-28](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953/); source video `youtu.be/n3jsYK_-aRU`, not watched)
- **GAP**: The exact Help Center definition of "viewed vs swiped away" (or "stayed to watch") and the number of seconds behind it. I could not load YouTube's Shorts tab, and third-party guides disagree on the label ([creatoressentials glossary](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/), not opened). The widely quoted "aim for 70–80 % viewed" has **no official source** ([bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks) says the same). Compare each Short against your own channel's baseline.
- **OFF**: For recommendations in general, YouTube's systems look at whether viewers choose to watch, ignore or click "not interested", then at "avg. view duration and avg. % viewed", then at likes and post-watch surveys. ([Search & discovery FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225)) The Shorts-specific tab did not render for me.
- **OFF**: Shorts can be up to 3 min (from 2024-10-15). **A Short over 1 min with any active Content ID claim is blocked globally and not recommended.** Most licensed songs are capped at 90 s, some at 60 or 30 s. ([Help 15424877](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/10/03/youtube-shorts-three-minutes-max-length-redesign-trends-templates/))
- **OFF (TikTok)**: "Whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end" is a strong signal. All signals are "weighted based on their value to a user". ([TikTok newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you/))
- **GAP**: Instagram Reels ranking on watch time, sends per reach and replays. It is widely attributed to Adam Mosseri, but I could not open a primary Meta page that says it.
- **OFF/DATA (LinkedIn)**: Full-screen vertical video reached the web in Feb 2025, and creators get average watch time. Uploads rose 36 % year on year. ([TechCrunch 2025-02-04](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/04/linkedin-amps-up-vertical-video-tools-as-uploads-jump-36)) There is no public retention research for LinkedIn vertical video: **GAP**.

## 2. When people swipe: the shape of the retention curve

- **PEER (systems venue)**: In a TikTok-like interface with 500 real TikTok videos, swipes cluster at the **start and at the end**. Among MTurk users, 29 % of swipes fell in the first 20 % of a video and 42 % in the last 20 %. Only 6 % fell between 60–80 % (college sample). Each video's swipe pattern was **stable across two different populations**, so swiping is mostly a property of the video, not the viewer. (n = 133 MTurk users / 15,344 swipes, plus 25 students / 3,069 swipes. Li, Xie, Netravali, Jamieson, "Dashlet", [arXiv 2204.12954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12954). I believe it appeared at NSDI '23; the opened page shows only the preprint.)
- **PEER**: Across 12 TikTok ads (~1.94 M impressions, mean length 56.7 s), **85 % of viewers who started were gone before the 25 % mark**. After that, roughly half of those remaining left in each later quarter. Churn did not correlate with CTR or CPC. One advertiser, so the sample of ads is small. (Salminen, Wahid, Yang, Jansen, HT '24, ACM; [open-access PDF](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstreams/e568795f-bef8-4663-bc9d-444feb27a09a/download))
- **VENDOR (TikTok)**: "90 % of ad recall impact" and "80 % of the awareness impact" are captured within 6 s. Source: TikTok's internal "Value of View" analysis, 2020/21, method unpublished. ([Creative Codes PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf))
- **VENDOR (Meta, 2016)**: People spent an average of 1.7 s on a piece of mobile feed content. In a rated sample of 850+ ads, only 23 % had a clear brand message in the first 10 s with the sound off. ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-releases-new-guide-to-help-advertisers-maximize-video-ad-performan/450818))
- **VENDOR**: Across ~508 k posts from 1,900+ creators, median Shorts view-through was ~45 % and the top 10 % reached ~91 %. 11–20 s Shorts had the highest median views. No breakdown of view-through by length was published. ([quso.ai, 2026-06](https://quso.ai/research/video-retention-benchmarks))
- **GAP**: "Humans have an 8-second (goldfish) attention span." It traces to a 2015 Microsoft Canada report citing "Statistic Brain", and attention researchers say it has no basis ([WSJ 2017, reprinted by UW-Madison](https://internet.psych.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/532-Master/532-UnitPages/Unit-09/Goldfish_MythBusting.pdf)). Do not justify design choices with it.

## 3. The opening 1–3 seconds (hooks)

- **PEER**: For internet video ads, **surprise and joy** concentrate attention and reduce zapping. The **level of surprise** mattered most for attention, and the **rate of increase of joy** mattered most for retention: a rising moment beats a flat "nice" one. Lab study with eye tracking and facial coding. (Teixeira, Wedel, Pieters 2012, *JMR* 49(2):144–159, [Tilburg record](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/emotion-induced-engagement-in-internet-video-ads/))
- **PEER**: A big, **central brand on screen increases zapping**; brand size alone does not. Short **brand "pulses"**, with the same total exposure, reduce avoidance. Viewers whose gaze is **dispersed** across the screen are more likely to zap. (Teixeira, Wedel, Pieters 2010, *Marketing Science* 29(5):783–804; eye tracking on 31 commercials, ~2,000 participants; [Tilburg record](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/moment-to-moment-optimal-branding-in-tv-commercials-preventing-av/))
- **PEER**: Entertainment has an **inverted-U** relationship with purchase intent. Entertainment **after** the brand appears helps; entertainment before it does not. (Teixeira, Picard, el Kaliouby 2014, *Marketing Science* 33(6); 82 ads, 178 consumers, webcam face tracking; [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v33y2014i6p809-827.html))
- **OFF (TikTok, correlational, method unpublished)**: Over 63 % of the highest-CTR ads put the key message or product in the first 3 s. Vertical shooting gives a 25 % higher 6-s watch-through. One in three top-VTR ads speak directly to camera. 40 % of top-VTR ads use text overlays. These are shares *of top performers* with no baseline, which is weak evidence. ([TikTok, 2020-10-21](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance))
- **VENDOR (TikTok/Lumen 2021)**: Suspense early gave +16 % watch time, surprise 1.7× VTR, and an emotional open 1.7× awareness. ([Creative Codes PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf))
- **VENDOR (TikTok/CreatorIQ 2023)**: A person in the first 2 s gave "+50 % hooking power" and +32 % ad recognition. Metric undefined. ([CreatorIQ](https://www.creatoriq.com/press/releases/tiktok-creatoriq-release-special-report-with-data-backed-keys-to-success-for-advertisers))
- **OFF (Google, Shorts ads)**: Jump straight into the action, add audio or text overlays, use bright high-contrast visuals and tight framing ("be unavoidable"), and "announce yourself with audio". ([Shorts ABCDs one-sheet](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/formarketingshortsabcdsonesheeters.pdf); [Google Ads blog 2024-04-17](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/youtube-shorts-ads-select-lineups-abcds/)) The ABCD framework's "+30 % short-term sales likelihood" is a **Kantar AI-model prediction** over 11,000 ads, not measured sales: **DATA (modelled)**. ([Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence))
- **ANEC**: Named hook types: open on the result, a bold claim, a question, "POV", or a cold open mid-action. No public test separates these types for organic Shorts: **GAP**. A paper that is said to analyse 46,605 TikTok hooks circulates on blogs, but I could not find it: **GAP**.

## 4. Curiosity gaps, open loops, narrative

- **PEER (theory)**: Curiosity is "cognitively induced deprivation" from a **perceived gap** in knowledge. It needs some prior knowledge, and it tends to **disappoint once satisfied**. (Loewenstein 1994, *Psych. Bulletin* 116(1):75–98; [abstract](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/))
- **PEER**: High curiosity improved recall of the answers (70.6 % vs 54.1 %, and 45.9 % vs 28.1 % a day later). The benefit to incidental material shown during the wait was small. Small n (19 in fMRI, ~28 in the follow-up). (Gruber, Gelman, Ranganath 2014, *Neuron* 84(2); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4252494/)) This suggests a question posed early and answered late helps viewers *remember* the answer. It does not prove retention in a feed.
- **OFF (TikTok)**: Suggested structure: hook (first 6 s), body, close. Narratives: "a-ha moments, problem-solutions, customer journey". ([Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf))
- **GAP**: The claim that "open loops/curiosity gaps raise Shorts retention by X %". No published test exists. The Dashlet result (most swipes happen early or at the end) is consistent with "the payoff holds people", but it does not test it.

## 5. Pacing, cuts, pattern interrupts

- **PEER**: More edits gave higher arousal and better memory for simple TV messages, with no extra cognitive load. (Lang, Zhou, Schwartz, Bolls, Potter 2000, *J. Broadcasting & Electronic Media* 44(1); [CityU record](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/the-effects-of-edits-on-arousal-attention-and-memory-for-televisi/))
- **PEER (model)**: In the limited-capacity model (LC4MP, Lang 2000), cuts trigger automatic **orienting responses**, which help with simple messages. With **complex messages**, the extra orienting responses take resources away from encoding and memory drops. Cuts between *unrelated* shots help less than related edits. ([summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LC4MP), secondary) Lang et al. 1999 (*Media Psychology*, "Something for nothing") found that fast pacing combined with arousing content lowered **verbal** recognition but not visual recognition. (Abstract seen only via search results: treat as secondary.)
- **PEER (descriptive)**: Hollywood shot-length sequences have drifted toward 1/f patterns that match how attention fluctuates. This describes *rhythm*, not "faster is better". (Cutting, DeLong, Nothelfer 2010, *Psych. Science*; [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/02/100223121435.htm))
- **VENDOR (TikTok/Neuro-Insight 2022)**: Fast scene changes "draw users in early", movement "catches the eye", and text pop-ups "sustain high engagement". No numbers given. ([Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf))
- **ANEC/GAP**: "Pattern interrupt every 2–3 s" and "a cut every 1.5 s". There is no source for these intervals. The theory behind them (orienting responses) is real, but the specific cadence is folklore.

## 6. On-screen text, voice and captions

- **DATA (survey, n = 5,616 US adults, 2019)**: 69 % watch with the sound off in public and 25 % even in private. 80 % of caption users are not hard of hearing. Captions raised ad recall by 8 %. A **logo plus captions together lowered ad memory by 12 %** on mobile. (Verizon Media/Publicis; [Streaming Media](https://www.streamingmedia.com/Articles/News/Online-Video-News/80-of-Video-Caption-Users-Arent-Hearing-Impaired-Finds-Verizon-131860.aspx)) This is pre-Shorts and pre-Reels.
- **VENDOR (TikTok)**: 88 % of users say sound is "vital" to TikTok (Nielsen 2020). ([TikTok blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/evolution-of-sound-volume-1)) TikTok and Reels are mostly watched with sound on, which conflicts with the 2019 sound-off figure. Platform context matters, and YouTube Shorts sound-on rates are not public: **GAP**.
- **PEER (review)**: More than 100 studies show same-language captions improve comprehension of, attention to and memory for video, especially for non-native viewers. (Gernsbacher 2015, *Policy Insights from BBS* 2(1); [PDF](https://successforkidswithhearingloss.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Video-Captions-Benefit-Everyone_2015.pdf)) The studies are about learning and TV, not feeds.
- **PEER (meta-analysis, 57 studies)**: Narration plus on-screen text beat narration alone, and **key terms beat verbatim text**. The advantage was largest when the material is system-paced (as in a Short) and for viewers with low prior knowledge. (Adesope & Nesbit 2012, *J. Ed. Psych.* 104(1); [WSU record](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Verbal-Redundancy-in-Multimedia-Learning-Environments/99900601157101842)) This is the evidence base for using **short keyword supers**, not karaoke transcripts, when diagrams are on screen.
- **OFF (YouTube Studio)**: Automatic captions are available in the Shorts editor. ([YouTube Creators – Shorts](https://www.youtube.com/creators/shorts/))

## 7. Faces versus motion graphics

- **PEER**: On TikTok, the presence of a human face raised engagement, and larger faces raised it more. Time on screen and hedonic versus utilitarian positioning moderate the effect. (Wallach et al. 2025, *J. Consumer Marketing* 42(3); 2,754 brand videos; [abstract](https://www.emerald.com/jcm/article-abstract/42/3/318/1247037/Analyzing-the-impact-of-faces-on-consumer?redirectedFrom=fulltext)) Engagement here means likes, comments and shares, not retention.
- **PEER**: In ~20,000 TikTok influencer ads, "what makes them engaging may have little to do with the product". **Product-specific engagement** (pixel-level attention on the product) predicted sales lift, and the effect was strongest for cheap, impulsive and hedonic products. (Yang, Zhang, Zhang 2025, *Marketing Science* 44(2); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormksc/v44y2025i2p247-267.html)) For a developer tool, the takeaway is to make the *product state* the thing people watch.
- **GAP**: Faces versus pure motion graphics for organic Shorts *retention*. I found no controlled comparison.

## 8. Length versus completion, loops and rewatch

- **OFF**: YouTube ranks on *both* average view duration and average % viewed ([FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225)). Sherman told creators to choose length by story, not by a target.
- **PEER (systems)**: Duration confounds watch-time models: longer videos earn more watch time mechanically, so recommender systems such as Kuaishou's correct for it. (Zhan et al., [arXiv 2206.06003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06003); the opened page shows only the preprint.) Comparing % viewed between a 15 s and a 50 s Short is therefore not like for like.
- **VENDOR**: Optimal-length claims of "11–30 s", "20–25 s" and "50–60 s" contradict each other and none publishes its method: **GAP**.
- **OFF**: Replays now count as views (see §1). Whether loops or rewatches raise *ranking* on YouTube is not documented: **GAP**. A seamless loop does push average % viewed above 100 %, which is a signal YouTube does name.

## 9. Sound and music

- **VENDOR (TikTok, 2020)**: Tracks "above 120 BPM" "often drive higher view-through rate". There is no data behind it. ([TikTok blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/9-creative-tips-to-drive-auction-ad-performance)) Kantar 2021 for TikTok reported a +120 % awareness lift for "sound-on" ad solutions, method unpublished ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/evolution-of-sound-volume-1)).
- **OFF (Google)**: "Announce yourself with audio", and coordinate sight and sound. ([one-sheet](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/formarketingshortsabcdsonesheeters.pdf))
- **OFF (constraint)**: Licensed music combined with a Short over 60 s risks a global block (§1).

## 10. Developer and technical audiences

- **DATA**: In the Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2024 (n = 48,822 for this question), 83.9 % learn from technical docs, 54.2 % from how-to videos, 49.9 % from video courses and 41.2 % from social media. ([survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2024/developer-profile/)) Developers use video to learn *how*. That says nothing about Shorts in particular.
- **OFF (Stack Overflow ads)**: Developers "will research your product or service first". eBooks and white-paper staples fit poorly. ([SO for marketers](https://stackoverflow.co/advertising/resources/stack-overflow-developer-survey-for-b2b-tech-marketers/insight-2/))
- **GAP**: Any peer-reviewed or large-sample study of how developers behave in short-form video feeds, including retention or hook types. I found none. "Developers hate hype" and "show the terminal" are **ANEC**, though Yang et al. (product engagement drives outcomes) and Adesope & Nesbit (key terms, low-prior-knowledge effect) point the same way.

## Decisions (testable rules)

1. **Judge every Short on engaged views, viewed-vs-swiped-away and average % viewed against the channel's own median, never on the view count.** Since 2026-08-24 the view count measures starts only. [OFF]
2. **Show the payoff state in frame 1.** The first frame must already show the product doing the thing (for a dev tool: a reviewed pull request or a passing check), not a logo or title card. Test: the swiped-away share falls compared with a version that opens on a logo. [OFF TikTok 63 %, PEER Teixeira 2010, VENDOR Meta 1.7 s]
3. **No centred logo in the opening seconds.** Brand in short pulses (small corner mark, a spoken name around 5–10 s, an end card) and keep the same total exposure. [PEER Teixeira 2010/2014]
4. **One focal point per beat.** Keep dispersed gaze down: one moving element, tight framing, high contrast, nothing competing in the first 2 s. [PEER Teixeira 2010 (dispersion predicts zapping), OFF Google "tight framing"]
5. **Open with a specific gap the viewer can name** ("why did the agent refuse to merge this?") and close it in the last third. The answer must be worth the wait, because satisfied curiosity tends to disappoint. [PEER Loewenstein, Gruber; GAP for feed retention]
6. **Build a rising moment, not a flat one:** at least one surprise beat and an upward turn in the last 25 %. The swipe peak at the end shows that viewers who stayed are deciding whether to rewatch. [PEER Teixeira 2012, PEER Dashlet]
7. **Cut on changes of meaning, not on a timer.** Use fast related edits for simple visual beats. Slow down while the voice delivers a technical claim, so verbal memory survives. [PEER Lang 2000 edits, LC4MP; ANEC/GAP for fixed cadences]
8. **Voice plus keyword supers, not full transcripts.** On-screen text shows 1–4 key words per beat. Burned-in captions only where no diagram competes, and never captions and logo fighting in the same moment. [PEER Adesope & Nesbit; DATA Verizon/Publicis (logo plus captions −12 % memory); PEER Gernsbacher]
9. **The first 3 s must be understandable with the sound off**, and the audio must still announce something when the sound is on. [DATA 2019 sound-off; OFF Google "announce with audio"; VENDOR TikTok sound-on]
10. **Choose the length by story beats; default to 20–45 s for a single idea and go longer only when the story needs it.** Never use licensed music on a Short over 60 s. [OFF Sherman, OFF Content ID rule; VENDOR length claims are weak]
11. **Make the product the visual subject, not just the "fun" around it.** Measure the share of frames in which the product state is the focal point. [PEER Yang et al. 2025]
12. **Engineer a clean loop** (last frame matches first) on one-idea Shorts, and track average % viewed above 100 %. [OFF replays count; GAP on ranking effect]
13. **Never pass scripted or deterministic demo footage off as live product use.** Credibility with sceptical technical viewers is the asset. [ANEC]

## Test these (weak or contested evidence)

- **Face versus no face in the first 2 s.** Founder on camera for 1.5 s, then the motion graphics, against motion graphics only. Metric: viewed-vs-swiped. Evidence: PEER on engagement only, VENDOR on hooks, nothing for developers.
- **Hook type.** Result-first ("this pull request was written, reviewed and blocked by agents") against question-first against a bold contrarian claim. The hook taxonomies are ANEC.
- **Length.** Cut the same script to ~20 s and ~45 s. Compare engaged views × average view duration, not % viewed alone, because duration confounds % viewed (PEER Zhan).
- **Cut cadence.** The same edit at roughly 1.5 s against 3 s average shot length on the technical middle section. LC4MP predicts that faster cuts hurt recall of complex claims.
- **Captions.** Full karaoke captions against keyword supers only. Measure retention and a 1-question recall poll in the comments or community tab.
- **Loop or end card.** A seamless loop against an end card with a CTA. Measure average % viewed and subscriber conversion.
- **Music tempo.** Above 120 BPM against below 90 BPM under identical picture. The only source is a TikTok blog line.
- **Platform transfer.** Post the identical cut to Shorts, LinkedIn vertical and TikTok and compare first-3-second retention. Sound-on norms differ by platform, and Shorts data on them is not public.
- **Method.** I did not verify whether YouTube offers any A/B split for Shorts *content*, as opposed to thumbnails or titles. Assume it does not: run each test as paired uploads a week apart at the same posting hour, and repeat each pair at least twice before believing a difference.
