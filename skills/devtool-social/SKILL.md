# Reach developers without sounding like an ad

Developers punish hype and reward a named pain. Stack Overflow's 2025 survey: 46 % distrust AI
output, favourable views fell from 72 % to 60 %, and the top frustration is "almost right, but not
quite". Content that says that out loud earns attention; superlatives lose it. Evidence labels and
sources: [research.md](research.md), and per topic [shorts.md](shorts.md), [humour.md](humour.md),
[brand.md](brand.md), [demand.md](demand.md), [law-eu.md](law-eu.md). Most attention and brand
research comes from ads, not organic Shorts or developers: treat it as transferred, and test it.

## Pick the job, then the platform

| Job | Format | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Strangers discover you | 20 to 45 s vertical clip, one tension, captions burned in | YouTube Shorts, LinkedIn native video |
| A buyer understands it | The full audience video, 16:9 | YouTube (unlisted links in sales mail), LinkedIn |
| Developers trust it | The thing itself: repo, chart, live demo | Show HN; subreddits whose rules allow it |
| A conversation starts | Founder text post with one honest opinion | LinkedIn personal profile (the `linkedin-post` skill) |

Show HN only takes what people can run or try; a video or a landing page is not a Show HN. Post
from an account with real comment history and stay in the thread for the first hours.

## Shorts people stay on

Swipes cluster in the first fifth and the last fifth of a video, and the pattern belongs to the
video, not the viewer ([shorts.md](shorts.md)).

1. **One tension per Short**, named in the viewer's own words: one buying situation, one claim.
2. **Frame one already shows the product doing the thing** (a reviewed pull request, a check
   failing, a budget stopping a run). No logo, no intro card, no centred logo at all early on.
3. **Say the product name in the first ~3 s**, off-centre and small on screen; then brief brand
   pulses (3 to 5 in 30 to 45 s), never one long hold.
4. **The first 3 s work muted**, and the audio announces something when sound is on.
5. **Keyword supers, not karaoke captions**, while a diagram is on screen; full captions only where
   nothing else competes. Never logo and captions fighting in the same moment.
6. **Cut on changes of meaning, not a timer**: fast for simple visual beats, slower while a
   technical claim is spoken. "A cut every 1.5 s" has no source.
7. **Open a gap the viewer can name and close it in the last third**, with a rising moment near
   the end. Default 20 to 45 s for one idea; longer only if the story needs it.
8. **Same end card every time** (name, tagline, sound, one call to action), or a clean loop.
9. Set the long video as the Short's related video. No licensed music on a Short over 60 s.

## Humour with an edge

Developers already distrust hype, so poking at it lands; being the hype makes you the target
([humour.md](humour.md)).

- **Targets**: the situation (a mild, specific, close pain), the hype vocabulary ("agentic",
  "swarms", "10x"), or the brand's unimportant traits. Never a group of people, a named company or
  person, job loss, security, correctness or cost.
- **Mock the word, not the technology.** The punchline is your product's real answer.
- **Signal the joke in the first 2 s, visually**: deadpan buzzword parody gets taken literally.
- **Prefer jokes the viewer has to get, with a true technical punchline**; they raise perceived
  competence, slapstick and put-downs do not. If the joke still works without the product, cut it.
- **Pay back the credibility in the same Short**: end on a real screen or a labelled demo.
- No rage bait, nothing that looks like a real notification or alert, no fake numbers. Test with
  five or more real developers first. Legal lines (EU): [law-eu.md](law-eu.md): exaggeration that
  is obviously not literal is fine; anything that sounds measured must be provable; a recognisable
  competitor, a real person's face or voice, or a parody of one specific ad needs a lawyer first.

## Recognition

Freeze four assets for a year and repeat them: the name (spoken and written), one logo sting, one
two-to-three-note sound that always plays with the name, one colour plus one typeface. Add one
recurring visual device that shows what the product does. Keep the look fixed and change the
argument per video; never refresh the look because you are tired of it. Test it: show developers a
muted one-second frame without the name and ask which product it is ([brand.md](brand.md)).

## Compose videos from parts

Audience videos repeat the same facts in different words. Keep them apart and compose:

| Part | Holds | Varies by audience? |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | One fact, its status (live or planned) and where it is proven | No; only its phrasing per register |
| Hook | One audience's pain or buying situation in their words | Yes; this is what you test |
| Scene | One fixed visual per claim, the same in every video | No; it is a brand asset |
| Recipe | Audience = hook + two or three claims + close | Yes |

A Short is one hook, one claim and the end card; a long video is the whole recipe.

## Cut a Short from a long video

Take the long video's strongest tension, rebuild its opening by the rules above, and post two or
three openings as separate Shorts. Shorts cannot use Test & Compare, so compare "stayed to watch"
against your own earlier Shorts and repeat a comparison before believing it.

## Launch a set of audience videos

1. Developer pieces first, with the humour pieces as Shorts: developers share what they recognise.
2. Then one client or business-role video per founder post on LinkedIn, each opening with that
   role's own question.
3. Leadership and vision pieces last, sent to people one by one rather than broadcast.
4. Answer every comment on day one. Questions from the roles you target are the success signal.

## Measure

View counts now count every start, so they say nothing about attention. Working means: stayed to
watch, engaged views and average % viewed rising against your own baseline, comments from the
target roles, and brand named from a frame.

**Demand** is behaviour on a ladder: visit, signup with one qualifying question, call, written
letter of intent or paid pilot ([demand.md](demand.md)). Shorts links are not clickable, so give
each audience its own short web address, spoken and on screen. Write the thresholds and kill
criteria down before the first video ships, keep a fixed window, and never move a threshold after
launch. Small traffic cannot A/B-test landing pages; compare each audience against its threshold.

## Gotchas

- Label every demo as recorded, planned features as planned, comparisons as illustrations. One
  exaggeration a developer catches costs the whole channel.
- Free-plan AI voice audio is non-commercial: never use it in public marketing.
- Other companies' logos only to show compatibility, unmodified, with a trademark line in the caption.
- Asking friends to upvote on Hacker News or Reddit is vote manipulation and gets the post killed.
- X, TikTok, Instagram Reels, Bluesky, Mastodon, dev.to, posting times and cadence have no evidence
  behind them here; present any rule about them as a test to run, not as fact.

Copy that must not read as generated: the `humanize` skill. Thumbnails, titles and the upload form:
the `youtube-upload-kit` skill. The videos themselves: the `seekframe` skill.
